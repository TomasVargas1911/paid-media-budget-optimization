# Multi-Platform Paid Media Optimization
### A Junior Marketer's Approach to Budget Strategy

> **Note:** This is a personal practice project. The data is simulated for
> learning and portfolio purposes it does not come from a live client
> account. The methodology, however, reflects the same process I'd apply
> to a real Google Ads account.

## Overview

As part of my growth as a junior digital marketer, I built this project to deepen my hands-on understanding of how performance marketing teams manage budgets and optimize campaigns across both **programmatic** (DV360, The Trade Desk) and **social/search** platforms (Meta Ads, Google Ads).

Rather than just studying the theory, I wanted to do the work — building budget models, running test campaigns, and learning to read the KPIs that actually drive decision-making in real agencies and in-house teams.

## The Challenge

Paid media budgets are rarely "set and forget." In real campaigns, teams have to constantly rebalance spend between channels based on what's actually converting — and doing that well requires understanding both the **strategic side** (where should money go?) and the **technical side** (how does each platform's auction and targeting actually work?).

This project simulates that full cycle end-to-end.

## My Approach

### 1. Budget Planning & Allocation
Designed a mock multi-channel budget split across DV360, TTD, Meta, and Google Ads, based on typical funnel-stage logic:
- **Programmatic channels** (DV360, TTD) → awareness/reach
- **Meta/Google Ads** → consideration and conversion

Practiced a weekly "check-in and reallocate" cadence, moving hypothetical budget toward whichever channel/segment showed the strongest early signals.

**Simulated monthly budget (mock account, $10,000 total):**

| Channel | Funnel Stage | Initial Allocation | Week 4 Allocation | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DV360 | Awareness | $3,000 (30%) | $2,400 (24%) | ▼ -$600 |
| The Trade Desk | Awareness | $2,000 (20%) | $1,600 (16%) | ▼ -$400 |
| Meta Ads | Consideration | $2,500 (25%) | $3,000 (30%) | ▲ +$500 |
| Google Ads (Search) | Conversion | $2,000 (20%) | $2,600 (26%) | ▲ +$600 |
| Google Ads (Display) | Consideration | $500 (5%) | $400 (4%) | ▼ -$100 |

**Reallocation logic:** budget moved from the two programmatic channels (weaker simulated CPA) toward Search and Meta, which showed better simulated conversion efficiency by week 3 — mirroring a real "double down on what's converting" decision.

### 2. Platform Familiarization
Spent time inside each platform's interface (demo accounts, free trials, and Google/Meta's own certification sandboxes) to understand:

| Platform | Focus Areas |
|---|---|
| **DV360 & TTD** | Audience targeting, deal IDs, viewability settings, programmatic bidding logic |
| **Meta Ads Manager** | Audience segmentation, creative testing, campaign objectives |
| **Google Ads** | Keyword strategy, Smart Bidding, Search vs. Display campaign structures |

### 3. Testing Methodology
Applied a basic test-and-learn framework: running small test variations of ad creative, copy, and audience targeting before "scaling" the winning variables — mimicking how real optimization cycles work.

**Simulated A/B test (Meta Ads, Week 2 — same budget and audience, creative variable only):**

| Variant | Spend | CTR | CPA | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Static image, benefit-led copy | $500 | 1.1% | $32.00 | Baseline |
| B — Short-form video, UGC style | $500 | 2.4% | $19.50 | **Winner → scaled to 70% of Meta budget** |

The winning variable (video/UGC format) was then applied across the remaining Meta budget for the rest of the month, which is reflected in the improved blended CTR and CPA in the KPI table above.

### 4. KPI Tracking & Interpretation
The core learning piece. Built a simple tracking spreadsheet to practice interpreting:

- **CPA** — cost efficiency per conversion
- **ROAS** — connecting spend directly to (simulated) revenue outcomes
- **CTR** — diagnosing creative and copy performance
- **CPM & Viewability** — relevant specifically to programmatic buying
- **Conversion Rate** — linking ad performance to landing page experience
- **Budget Pacing** — ensuring spend tracks correctly against timeline, avoiding under- or overspend

**Simulated KPI results by channel (Month 1):**

| Channel | Spend | Impressions | CTR | CPM | Conversions | CPA | ROAS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DV360 | $2,400 | 1,200,000 | 0.35% | $2.00 | 48 | $50.00 | 1.4x |
| The Trade Desk | $1,600 | 800,000 | 0.32% | $2.00 | 29 | $55.17 | 1.3x |
| Meta Ads | $3,000 | 600,000 | 1.8% | $5.00 | 120 | $25.00 | 3.1x |
| Google Ads (Search) | $2,600 | 130,000 | 4.2% | $20.00 | 156 | $16.67 | 4.6x |
| Google Ads (Display) | $400 | 200,000 | 0.6% | $2.00 | 8 | $50.00 | 1.2x |
| **Total / Blended** | **$10,000** | **2,930,000** | **1.1% avg** | — | **361** | **$27.70** | **3.0x** |

**Weekly budget pacing (simulated, $10,000 monthly cap):**

| Week | Planned Spend | Actual Spend | Pacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | $2,500 | $2,380 | -4.8% (under) |
| Week 2 | $2,500 | $2,610 | +4.4% (over) |
| Week 3 | $2,500 | $2,490 | -0.4% (on track) |
| Week 4 | $2,500 | $2,520 | +0.8% (on track) |

**Key takeaways from the simulated data:**
- Search and Meta drove the strongest ROAS, justifying the mid-flight reallocation away from programmatic.
- Programmatic channels (DV360/TTD) delivered lower CPM and strong reach, but weaker down-funnel efficiency — consistent with their intended awareness role.
- Pacing stayed within ±5% of plan every week, avoiding the under-delivery/overspend risk common with manual budget management.

## What I Learned

This project taught me that successful paid media management isn't just about picking the "best" platform — it's about understanding how channels work together across the funnel, and having the discipline to let **data, not assumptions**, guide budget decisions.

It also gave me practical, hands-on familiarity with tools that are standard in performance marketing roles, which I'm now looking to apply in a real team environment.
